Teaching & Academic Programs

My teaching bridges criminological theory, spatial methodology, and practical data analytics. I aim to equip students with critical analytical thinking and technical competencies to examine spatial crime patterns, neighborhood dynamics, and criminal justice policies using empirical methods and urban data analytics.

Program Leadership & Curriculum Coordination

🔗 Official Program Page ↗

I direct and coordinate the Undergraduate Certificate in Crime and Intelligence Analysis in the College of Criminology and Criminal Justice at Florida State University. This specialized program trains students in analytical problem-solving, tactical intelligence, and spatial analytics for careers in law enforcement, public safety, and crime intelligence agencies.

CCJ 4742 Certificate Required Course
GIS Applications in Criminology

A core hands-on laboratory course for the Certificate Program. Introduces foundational GIS principles, spatial data handling, point pattern analysis, kernel density hotspot mapping, and practical software applications using ArcGIS / QGIS.

Core Requirement GIS Laboratory Hotspot Mapping
CJE 4732 Certificate Required Course
Introduction to Crime Analysis

The primary foundational requirement for the Certificate Program. Covers tactical, operational, and strategic crime analysis, analytical problem-solving models (SARA), pattern detection, and data-driven law enforcement decision-making.

Core Requirement Tactical & Strategic Analysis Data-Driven Policing
CCJ 4070 Certificate Elective Course
Crime Hotspots

A key theoretical elective for the Certificate Program. Explores environmental criminology, crime pattern theory, and place-based crime concentration, focusing on why crime clusters in specific micro-places and how situational crime prevention is structured.

Certificate Elective Environmental Criminology Place-Based Prevention

Other Undergraduate Courses

CCJ 4746 Major Required Course
Statistics in Criminology

A core quantitative methods requirement for the criminology major. Covers foundational statistical concepts, hypothesis testing (t-test, z-test), Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), bivariate correlation, and ordinary least squares (OLS) linear regression for empirical research.

Major Requirement Applied Statistics Hypothesis Testing Linear Regression
CCJ 4031
Individual and Society: City, Neighborhood, and Crime

Investigates urban sociology, neighborhood structural characteristics, and crime. Topics range from foundational urban theories (e.g., Chicago School, Social Disorganization) to contemporary spatial and neighborhood-level crime research.

Urban Sociology Neighborhood & Community Spatial Inequality
