Teaching

My teaching integrates criminological theory with spatial methodologies and quantitative data analysis. I aim to equip students, from undergraduates learning fundamentals to graduate researchers developing advanced studies, with critical thinking and computational skills to rigorously examine crime patterns and urban problems.

Graduate Courses & Seminars

CCJ 5XXX / CCJ 6XXX
Advanced Spatial Analysis & GIS in Criminology

A graduate-level seminar focused on spatial econometrics, point pattern modeling, spatial lag/error specifications, and computational research methods in environmental criminology and spatial social science.

Spatial Econometrics Advanced GIS Quantitative Methodology
Undergraduate

Undergraduate Courses

CCJ 4070
Crime Hotspots

An upper-level theoretical seminar exploring environmental criminology, crime pattern theory, and place-based perspectives. Students examine why crime concentrates in specific places and evaluate situational crime prevention strategies.

Environmental Criminology Place-Based Crime Crime Concentration
CCJ 4031
Individual and Society

Investigates the intersection of urban sociology, neighborhood structural characteristics, and crime. Topics range from foundational urban theories (e.g., Chicago School, Social Disorganization) to contemporary spatial and neighborhood-level crime research.

Urban Sociology Neighborhood & Community Spatial Inequality
CCJ 4742
GIS Applications in Criminology

A hands-on laboratory course introducing the fundamentals of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and spatial data analysis. Students learn cartographic principles, point pattern analysis, kernel density estimation, and practical GIS software operations.

GIS Laboratory Spatial Data Analytics ArcGIS / QGIS
CJE 4732
Introduction to Crime Analysis

Provides a comprehensive overview of tactical, operational, and strategic crime analysis. Focuses on data collection, pattern detection, analytical problem-solving (SARA model), and data-driven criminal justice decision making.

Crime Analysis Tactical & Strategic Analysis Evidence-Based Policing
CCJ 4746
Statistics in Criminology

An introduction to quantitative analysis in criminal justice research, covering descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing (t-test, z-test), Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), bivariate correlation, and ordinary least squares (OLS) linear regression.

Applied Statistics Hypothesis Testing Linear Regression
CCJ 3011
Criminology

A foundational survey course examining core criminological theories, patterns and trends of criminal behavior, definitions of crime, and societal responses to crime and delinquency.

Criminological Theory Crime Trends Core Curriculum
